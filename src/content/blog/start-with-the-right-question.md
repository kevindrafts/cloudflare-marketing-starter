The first conversation about a website often starts with pages, features, and launch dates. Those details matter, but there is a more useful place to begin: what should be different when this work is done?

## Find the underlying need

“We need a new website” describes an output. “Our customers don’t understand what we do” describes a problem. The second statement gives you something to investigate, and a way to recognize progress.

## Make room for a real answer

Talk to the people who use your service. Ask what surprised them, where they got stuck, and what made them trust you. Their words are often more useful than a room full of assumptions.

## Write a smaller brief

A useful brief can fit on a page. Describe your audience, the problem, the action you want them to take, and how you’ll know the work helped. Let those decisions guide everything else.
